# Fiction continuity audit

## Scope and anchors

Confirm the current manuscript version and whether review or rewriting is requested. Do not choose “latest modified” as authoritative when several versions exist. Preserve paragraph numbers, scene/chapter names and page anchors before extracting.

Do not attribute fictional dialogue or narration to the user's real biography or beliefs.

## Build only the ledgers needed

- Timeline: explicit dates, travel durations, age and before/after constraints.
- Character state: presence, injuries, objects, relationships and relevant abilities.
- Knowledge: who learned which fact, from whom, and when.
- Viewpoint: whose perceptions the current scene permits.

Separate hard contradictions, plausible ambiguities, stylistic concerns and intentional unreliable narration. A narrator changing their mind is not automatically a continuity error.

## Investigate each finding

Locate both conflicting passages. Quote short necessary excerpts with precise anchors. Explain the violated constraint and confidence. Check nearby context for a reveal, flashback, scene break or explicit exception before flagging it.

Offer the smallest local remedy as a suggestion. Do not rewrite surrounding chapters to make your diagnosis true. For returning characters, check entrance/exit and what happened while absent.

## Author decisions

At major relationship, fate, plot or ending forks, ask one clear decision. Do not prewrite the ending as a convenience. A request to pause means preserve state and resume by reviewing the last agreed findings.

## Deliver

Group actionable findings by severity; each needs source anchor, contradiction/ambiguity class and minimal proposal. “No hard issue found in reviewed sections” is narrower and more honest than “the whole novel is perfect.”

Acceptance example: two passages place a character in incompatible locations at the same time; both anchors are supplied and an author-selectable correction is proposed without silently modifying the manuscript.
